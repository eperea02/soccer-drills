Pull-Back Relay

A relay race built entirely around one move — the pull-back — repeated until it's second nature.

Two lines race side by side through a short row of cones, but there's a catch: at every cone, the ball carrier has to plant, sole-roll the ball backward, and change direction before continuing. Speed doesn't matter if the pull-back is sloppy — clean technique is what gets a team through the course fastest.

The setup

  • Kid dribbling
  • Cone marking a pull-back point
  • Zig-zag path through the course
  • Direction of travel

How to run it

  1. Split the group into two even lines. Set out 3–4 cones in a straight line in front of each team, spaced a few yards apart.
  2. First kid in each line dribbles to the first cone.
  3. At the cone, they plant a foot, use the sole to roll the ball backward, and pivot to change direction (a pull-back).
  4. They weave to the next cone and pull back again, continuing down the whole line of cones.
  5. Once past the last cone, they sprint the ball straight back to the front of the line.
  6. They tag the next teammate, who repeats the course — first line to get everyone through wins.

Coaching tips

  • Start slow with no relay/racing pressure — get the pull-back mechanics clean first, then add the race.
  • Exaggerate the sole-roll at first so it's a real change of direction, not just a stutter step.
  • Works well as a pure technique station too, without ever turning it into a race.
A whole practice's worth of pull-backs disguised as a race — nobody counts reps when there's a line to beat.

Soccer warm-up · all ages · 5–10 min · any group size

Box Dribbling

Dribble, freeze, trick, repeat. The simplest way to sneak a whole checklist of ball-mastery moves into a warm-up.

Cone off a box and let everyone loose with a ball, dribbling wherever there's space. Every so often, blow the whistle and call out a quick move — toe touches, a pull-back, a juggle, a trap. Everyone freezes their dribble, does the move in place, and goes right back to weaving around the box. No elimination, no waiting — just touches on the ball, over and over, with a surprise every whistle.

How to run it

  1. Cone off a box — big enough that touches don't turn into a pile-up, small enough to keep it a warm-up, not a jog.
  2. Every kid grabs a ball and dribbles anywhere inside the box, under close control.
  3. At a random moment, blow the whistle and call out a move from the list below.
  4. Everyone freezes their dribble on the spot and performs that move — a set number of reps, or until you say "go."
  5. Back to free dribbling until the next whistle.
  6. Keep the timing unpredictable and rotate through moves so nobody can just wait for their favorite.

Trick menu

Call out whichever fits your group's level — mix easy ones in with a couple of stretch moves.

  • 5 toe touches
  • Pull-back
  • Sole roll
  • Inside-outside
  • Juggle x3
  • Trap & go
  • Scissor
  • V-pull
  • Stop & go

Coaching notes

  • Start with moves everyone can already do, then work in one new skill per session — the whistle format is a great place to introduce something you're teaching that week.
  • Keep the box on the smaller side once touches look solid — less room means more forced close control.
  • Whistle more often than feels natural at first. The value is in the repetition, not any single move.
  • Throw in an occasional "freeze, look up" whistle with no trick attached — it's a cheap way to build the habit of scanning the field.
Works just as well as a circle if a square is harder to pace out — the whistle doesn't care about the shape either.

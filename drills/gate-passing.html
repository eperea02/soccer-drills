Gate Passing

Scatter a few cone gates around the grid and let pairs rack up points passing the ball through them.

Set a handful of small cone "gates" around an open grid. Split into pairs, one ball each. A pass that goes through any gate and reaches your partner scores a point — and because there's more than one gate open at a time, pairs have to look up, pick a target, and adjust on the fly instead of just booting it back and forth.

The setup

  • Kid passing
  • Direction of the pass
  • Cone marking a gate
  • The ball in flight through a gate

How to play

  1. Each pair spreads out inside the grid with their ball.
  2. One partner passes the ball through any open gate to the other partner.
  3. A pass that goes through a gate and reaches the partner scores a point for the pair.
  4. Pairs can use any open gate, not just the closest one — encourage them to move around and mix it up.
  5. Play for a set time and see how many points each pair racks up.
  6. Progression: once the basics click, add one or two defenders who try to block passes through the gates.

Coaching tips

  • For 6-year-olds, keep the gates wide and close together at first — accuracy comes with reps, not tight gates.
  • Call out "switch gates!" every so often to keep pairs moving instead of camping on one gate.
  • Save the defender variation for a later session once basic passing accuracy is solid.
Simple math, endless replay value — more gates than balls means somebody's always got a lane open.
